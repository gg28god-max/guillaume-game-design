Outpost Red — Concept Art Case Study

The Other Half of This Project

Concept art only. The UI/UX system lives on the UI/UX portfolio.

View the UI/UX of Outpost Red →

02 — Art Brief

The Brief

Outpost Red asked for concept art grim enough to sell a contamination event without a single line of UI to lean on — the rover, the suit, and the crew themselves had to carry the whole story. The brief below is what I held that work to.

Premise

A concept art case study set in the aftermath of an extraterrestrial contamination event on a long-range Martian expedition — sterile aerospace technology set against visceral, bioluminescent infection, carried across a mechanical rover, a ruptured space suit, and the crew themselves.

Mood & Tone

Hard-sci-fi survival horror — a desaturated Martian palette broken only by a hazard-orange accent, so the technology reads as clinical and the infection reads as wrong. Isolation first, monster second.

Deliverables

Vehicle design — a mechanical space rover pushed through multiple render passes and terrain contexts — alongside full character design for the contaminated crew member, from silhouette through final render.

Process & Tools

Blender for base forms and camera-accurate proportions, Photoshop for paint-over and final render. Real aerospace and hazmat references grounded every design pass, so the hardware reads as plausible before the infection ever touches it.

03 — The Problem

The Problem I Set Out to Solve

Making body horror read as still human

The contaminated crew member had to be horrifying without stopping being a person in a suit. The bioluminescent growth needed to overtake the body without erasing the silhouette underneath — the helmet, the harness, the boots all stay legible, because the horror is in what's still recognizably human, not in what isn't.

Grounding the tech so the horror lands

The rover and the suit had to read as plausible hardware first — real panel lines, real hazmat and aerospace references — so that the infection overtaking them reads as a violation of something functional, not just texture painted over a shape.

One visual language across vehicle and character

The same aerospace-meets-infection tone had to hold whether I was painting the rover's hull or the crew member's ruptured suit, so the two pieces read as one fiction — the same contamination, the same light, the same desaturated palette — not two unrelated studies.
