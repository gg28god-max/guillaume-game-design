3D Asset Creation · Technical Concept Art · 2025

Tide Hollow

A deep dive into asset creation and scene assembly focused on translating original worldbuilding into production-ready 3D assets. Employing a rigorous high-to-low poly workflow, each piece moves from sculpted high-resolution detail through retopology, UV unwrapping, PBR texturing in Substance Painter, and final scene assembly in Unreal Engine 5.

Year

2025

Role

3D Artist & Concept Artist

Tools

Blender, ZBrush, Substance Painter, Unreal Engine 5

Disciplines

3D Modelling, PBR Texturing, World-Building, Scene Assembly

02 — Art Brief

The Brief

Tidehollow started from a single question: what does architecture look like when a culture designs around the tide instead of against it? The brief below is the one I set for myself before opening ZBrush — the parameters everything downstream had to answer to.

Premise

A self-sustaining coastal fishing settlement built in harmony with tidal rhythms — maritime eco-fantasy, adventure/exploration tone, an original concept with no existing IP to reference.

Mood & Tone

Cultural resilience over crisis. Weathered, salt-crusted, fog-laden — the sea as a constant presence, not a threat. Warm firelight against a cool night palette.

Deliverables

One hero environment (the stilted settlement), a modular architecture kit (the Silt Pod dwelling, built to read at multiple scales), and a character/prop breakdown (the Tide Fisher) proving the world's objects have function, not just silhouette.

Pipeline & Constraints

Full high-to-low production pipeline, solo: ZBrush sculpt → retopology → UV → Substance Painter PBR texturing → Unreal Engine 5 scene assembly and lighting. Every asset had to survive that whole chain, not just look good as a sculpt.

03 — The Problem

The Problem I Set Out to Solve

Making an invented culture feel lived-in, not decorative

Concept art can get away with a beautiful silhouette. A production-ready asset can't — the conical hat has to actually shed rain, the shoulder guard has to actually cushion a net's weight. Every design decision on the Tide Fisher and the Silt Pod dwelling had to answer "why does this exist" before it answered "does this look interesting."

Solo, full-pipeline execution

Nobody hands off between departments here. The same hands that sculpted high-resolution detail in ZBrush had to retopologize it clean enough to texture, texture it accurately enough to read as PBR materials, and light it well enough to hold up in a real-time engine. A weak link anywhere in that chain shows up in the final render.

Communicating adaptation without a single line of dialogue

The brief called for "cultural resilience and ecological adaptation" — but this is an environment project, not a narrative one. No cutscene, no text, no NPC explaining the lore. The stilted foundations, the salt-crusted adobe, and the fish drying in open air all had to carry that theme on their own, through material choices and construction logic a viewer could read at a glance.
