Concept Art · Character Design · Illustration · 2020

Diverse Creative Studies

A technical showcase of early design range spanning a broad spectrum of subjects — from original character concepts and heavy mechs, to submersible vehicles, prehistoric riding creatures, and fantasy warriors. This collection demonstrates versatility across styles, disciplines, and subject matter, reflecting the breadth of a practice grounded in rigorous observation and imaginative world-building.

Year

2020

Role

Concept Artist & Illustrator

Disciplines

Character Design, Mechanical Design, Creature Design, Illustration

02 — Art Brief

The Brief

Nobody commissioned Diverse Creative Studies. I set my own brief: prove range across hard-surface, organic, and character design without the collection reading as a pile of disconnected practice pieces. The brief below is what I held myself to.

Premise

A self-directed technical showcase spanning vehicles, mechs, creatures, and characters — a personal collection rather than a client brief, built to demonstrate design range under one consistent process.

Mood & Tone

Deliberately no single mood — cold sci-fi blueprint precision for the Barracuda submersible, warmer painterly rendering for the character studies, because the range itself is the point of the collection.

Deliverables

Original character concepts (Kuzok's second-in-command, pushed through three distinct costume iterations), a heavy mech (Big Papa), a submersible vehicle, a prehistoric riding creature, and a fantasy archer — each subject carried through multiple design passes.

Pipeline & Constraints

Orthographic drafting discipline held constant across every subject: silhouette first, then a material pass, then value structure, then final render — entirely in Photoshop — so wildly different genres would still share one visible working method.

03 — The Problem

The Problem I Set Out to Solve

Proving range without looking scattered

A portfolio spanning a submersible, a mech, and a fantasy warrior risks reading as unfocused. The fix wasn't picking one genre — it was keeping the same silhouette-first process visible in every piece, so the range reads as versatility rather than indecision.

Grounding fantasy in the same logic as hard-surface

Big Papa Mecha and the Kuzok costume studies sit in completely different genres, but both had to obey the same rule: every design choice traceable back to a functional or anatomical reason, not just an aesthetic one. That's what keeps a fantasy character and a mechanical robot feeling like they were designed by the same hand.

Iterating in public

The Kuzok's second-in-command sheet keeps three visibly different costume passes side by side instead of hiding the process behind a single final image. For a self-directed study, the iteration itself is part of what's being demonstrated — not just the destination, but how I got there.
